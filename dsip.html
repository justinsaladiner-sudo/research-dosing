DSIP

Delta sleep-inducing peptide

Catalog: DSIP · Neuropeptides and cognitive references · Evidence ceiling C

A Approved clinical use   B Human interventional   C Human observational / expert   D Preclinical   E Community / vendor   N/E Insufficient evidence

Mechanism

  • COriginally reported to promote delta (slow-wave) sleep. No receptor has been found; a 2006 review called it 'a still unresolved riddle'.1

Identity

ClassNonapeptide first isolated from rabbit brain (1970s)
TargetsNo specific receptor identified
Regulatory statusNot approved anywhere.
Supplier formulationLyophilized research powder; 5 mg vial (catalog).

Intended effects

  • CInsomnia / sleep quality1

Secondary findings

    Human evidence

    • CSmall 1980s trials (e.g. 16 chronic insomniacs, IV, 4 nights) reported better sleep efficiency and less wakefulness; other studies found weak or inconsistent effects.1
    • CProbably fewer than 200 people have been exposed across all published human studies.1

    Preclinical findings

      Documented dosing & cycles

      E Catalog protocol (researchdosing.com)2

      • 5 mg + 2.5 mL BAC water
      • 100 mcg (week 1) → 200 mcg (week 2) → +100 mcg steps as needed (up to 700 mcg)
      • Daily, 30–60 min before bed
      • 8–12 week cycle, then 4–8 week washout

      Preserved from the catalog as written. This is a vendor protocol, not a clinical recommendation.

      Optimization & support

      Foundational support

      BProtect slow-wave sleep3
      Why
      The largest natural GH pulse comes in early slow-wave sleep. That's the physiology bedtime GH-secretagogue dosing tries to ride.
      Mechanism
      Slow-wave sleep and nocturnal GH release are tightly coupled. Both decline with age.
      Basis
      Inferred: GH physiology. Not tested as an add-on to this compound.
      Downside
      None.

      Mechanistic support

      Complementary interventions

      BMelatonin before bed4
      Why
      The evidence-backed sleep-onset aid, for compounds marketed for sleep.
      Mechanism
      MT1/MT2 receptor agonist; shifts and signals the circadian night.
      Basis
      Meta-analysis of 19 RCTs: falls asleep ~7 min faster; larger effects with higher dose and longer use. Not tested with DSIP, Pinealon or Epitalon.
      Downside
      Modest effect; next-day grogginess possible; stacking several sleep agents compounds sedation.

      A plausible mechanism doesn't prove synergy. Nothing here is called synergistic without combination data.

      Redundancies & overlap
