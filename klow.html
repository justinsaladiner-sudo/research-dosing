      Optimization & support

      Foundational support

      AVitamin C sufficiency (RDA 75 mg women / 90 mg men)3
      Why
      Collagen can't be made properly without vitamin C.
      Mechanism
      Cofactor for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases that stabilize collagen.
      Basis
      Established nutrition science (NIH ODS). Extra beyond sufficiency isn't shown to boost repair-peptide effects.
      Downside
      Very high doses can cause GI upset and raise kidney-stone risk in susceptible people (NIH ODS).

      Mechanistic support

      BGelatin 15 g + vitamin C (~50 mg), 1 hour before training / rehab loading4
      Why
      Feeds collagen building blocks to tendons and ligaments right when loading stimulates repair.
      Mechanism
      Gelatin raises blood glycine, proline and hydroxyproline (peak ~1 h); vitamin C is the cofactor for collagen hydroxylation; brief loading drives connective-tissue uptake.
      Basis
      Demonstrated for a collagen-synthesis marker (≈ doubled) in 8 men (Shaw 2017). Not tested with any repair peptide; no injury-outcome data.
      Downside
      Small study; a marker, not healing. Gelatin adds ~60 kcal per 15 g.
      BHydrolyzed collagen peptides daily5
      Why
      The best-supported oral supplement for skin hydration and elasticity.
      Mechanism
      Collagen-derived peptides (e.g. Pro-Hyp) are absorbed and signal fibroblasts; also supply amino acids for matrix.
      Basis
      Meta-analyses of 19–26 RCTs: better hydration and elasticity; wrinkle reduction not robust. Not tested with GHK-Cu or other peptides.
      Downside
      Benefit builds over weeks; duration mattered more than dose.

      Complementary interventions

      A plausible mechanism doesn't prove synergy. Nothing here is called synergistic without combination data.
