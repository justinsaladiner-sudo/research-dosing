What is NAD+?

NAD+ is a coenzyme every cell uses to make energy and repair DNA, and levels fall with age. IV infusions are popular in clinics, but plasma levels barely move in the first hours, and the only human data are a small pharmacokinetic pilot. Under-the-skin NAD+ hasn't been studied.

Clinical evidence

  • CPilot PK study: 6-hour IV infusion (≈750 mg)
  • CIV NAD+ commonly causes flushing, nausea, chest tightness and cramping at faster infusion rates (clinic and industry reports).
  • N/ENo human study of SC NAD+ was found.

Common side effects

  • Flushing, nausea, cramping
  • Chest tightness (IV, rate-related)
  • Injection-site discomfort

Important considerations

  • Flushing, nausea, chest tightness and cramping are common with IV NAD+, and rate-related
  • Catalog storage note: use mixed NAD+ within 30–45 days, shorter than most peptides

Reconstitution example

Add 2.5 mL BAC water → 200 mg/mL

  1. Swab both vial tops with an alcohol pad
  2. Draw the BAC water (3 mL syringe, 22–23G)
  3. Inject slowly into the peptide vial
  4. Roll gently between your palms; don't shake
  5. Let it sit 15–30 min until fully dissolved
