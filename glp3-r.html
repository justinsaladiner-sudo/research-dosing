01

Retatrutide

Triple GIP / GLP-1 / glucagon receptor agonist

Catalog: GLP3-R · Metabolic compounds · Evidence ceiling B

A Approved clinical use   B Human interventional   C Human observational / expert   D Preclinical   E Community / vendor   N/E Insufficient evidence

Mechanism

  • DFull agonist at all three receptors. In vitro cAMP potency vs native ligands: ≈8.9× more potent than GIP at GIPR, ≈2.5× less potent than GLP-1 at GLP-1R, ≈2.9× less potent than glucagon at GCGR. (Figures come via secondary reproduction of the paper's tables.)6
  • DGLP-1R: central appetite suppression, slower gastric emptying, glucose-dependent insulin secretion.6
  • DGIPR: added insulinotropic effect, and possibly tolerability of GLP-1-driven nausea. The mechanism isn't settled in humans.6
  • DGCGR: raises energy expenditure and hepatic fat oxidation in rodents. The insulin-secretion arms are thought to offset glucagon's tendency to raise blood glucose.6

Identity

Supplier formulationLyophilized research powder; vials 10 / 30 / 40 / 60 / 120 mg (catalog). Not the clinical-trial drug product.

Intended effects

  • BWeight reduction in obesity / overweight with comorbidity1
  • BGlycemic control in type 2 diabetes2
  • BLiver fat reduction (MASLD)3

Secondary findings

  • BLiver fat (MASLD substudy, n=98, ≥10% liver fat by MRI-PDFF): relative change at 24 wk −42.9% (1 mg), −57.0% (4 mg), −81.4% (8 mg), −82.4% (12 mg) vs +0.3% placebo. 79–86% reached <5% liver fat on 8–12 mg.3
  • BDose-dependent heart-rate increase that peaks around week 24, then declines. Thought to come from the GLP-1/glucagon chronotropic effect.1
  • BCutaneous hyperesthesia / skin sensitivity: 7% vs 1% placebo (phase 2). None severe; mechanism unknown.1
  • BKnee osteoarthritis pain and function improved alongside weight loss (TRIUMPH-4, topline).5

Human evidence

  • BPhase 2 obesity (n=338, 48 wk, SC weekly): −24.2% at 12 mg vs −2.1% placebo. On 12 mg, 100% / 93% / 83% reached ≥5% / ≥10% / ≥15% loss. No plateau by week 48. Lilly-funded.1
  • BPhase 2 T2D (on metformin): HbA1c −2.02% at 24 wk on 12 mg vs −1.41% dulaglutide 1.5 mg and −0.01% placebo. Weight up to −16.9% at 36 wk.2
  • BTRIUMPH-1 phase 3 (n=2,339, no diabetes, 80 wk): −19.0% (4 mg), −25.9% (9 mg), −28.3% (12 mg). −30.3% at 104 wk in the BMI ≥35 extension (n=532).4
  • BTRIUMPH-2 (T2D, 80 wk): Lilly topline −12.7% / −19.1% / −20.8% at 4 / 9 / 12 mg, A1C down up to 1.6%. The EASD 2026 presentation reported 11.9–18.8%, likely a different estimand; the exact basis isn't confirmed.5
  • BTRIUMPH-3 (severe obesity + established CVD, 80 wk): up to −22.6%. MACE occurred less often than anticipated (not an outcomes trial).5
  • BLimitation: phase 3 results are company-reported topline; peer-reviewed full papers were pending at capture. All trials were sponsor-funded.5

Preclinical findings

  • DRodent models: weight loss beyond GLP-1/GIP dual agonism, which the authors attribute to GCGR-driven energy expenditure. The energy-expenditure share hasn't been quantified in humans.6

Per protocol, escalation ran every 4 weeks over 12 weeks from a 2 mg (or 4 mg) start. The 2→4→8→12 step order is read from the dose arms. A 2 mg start gave fewer GI events than a 4 mg start. Phase 3 maintenance doses were 4, 9 and 12 mg.

E Catalog protocol (researchdosing.com)10

  • GLP-naive: start at 2 mg per week
  • Prior GLP-1 use with at least one dose increase: start at 4 mg per week
  • Every 7 days, same day each week; no fasting needed
  • Cycle optional: 4 months on, 4 months off
  • No escalation schedule given; unit tables run to 12 mg

Preserved from the catalog as written. This is a vendor protocol, not a clinical recommendation.

Catalog discrepancies (flagged, record unchanged)

  • Start dose: the catalog uses 4 mg for GLP-experienced users. Phase 2 found more GI events with a 4 mg start than a 2 mg start.
  • The catalog gives no escalation schedule. The trials used 4-week steps to 12 mg (phase 2) and 4 / 9 / 12 mg maintenance (phase 3).
  • Catalog cycling (4 on / 4 off) has no trial basis.
  • Unit math: every unit value the catalog prints matches the computed value (see the table).

Optimization & support

Foundational support

CProtein 1.2–1.6 g/kg/d during active loss7
Why
GLP-1-class drugs cut energy intake by 16–39%, so lean mass is at risk.
Mechanism
Supplies amino acids for muscle protein synthesis during an energy deficit. Base the target on adjusted body weight or fat-free mass, not actual weight. Avoid ≥2 g/kg/d long term.
Basis
Inferred: joint advisory for GLP-1 therapy. Not tested with retatrutide.
Downside
Protein above muscle needs may convert to fat. Protein/fat-heavy meals slow gastric emptying and can worsen constipation.
BResistance training ≥3×/wk + ≥150 min/wk aerobic7
Why
Protein alone is unlikely to preserve muscle without structured strength training.
Mechanism
Mechanical loading signals muscle to keep lean and bone mass during rapid weight loss. Aerobic work alone preserves less.
Basis
Inferred: weight-loss trials + GLP-1 retrospective data. No retatrutide body-composition trial found.
Downside
None specific. Ramp up gradually if intake is very low.
CHydration + electrolytes7
Why
Nausea, vomiting and diarrhea are common (vomiting 25% at 12 mg).
Mechanism
GI fluid loss leads to volume depletion. Postmarket GLP-1 reports link this to renal impairment around initiation and dose escalation.
Basis
Inferred: GLP-1 class postmarket reports via the advisory.
Downside
Watch renal function if renal impairment is present.
CMicronutrient sufficiency (consider vitamin D, calcium, B12, multivitamin-mineral)7
Why
At intakes under ≈1,200 kcal/d (women) or 1,800 kcal/d (men), deficiencies become likely.
Mechanism
Nutrients of concern: iron, calcium, magnesium, zinc, and vitamins A, D, E, K, B1, B12, C. Warning signs: unusual fatigue, hair loss, poor wound healing.
Basis
Inferred: GLP-1 class advisory.
Downside
Dose supplements to need. Fat-soluble vitamins can accumulate.

Mechanistic support

CFiber, magnesium citrate, PEG 3350 for constipation7
Why
Constipation reached 26% at 12 mg (TRIUMPH-1).
Mechanism
Slower gastric and GI transit is an on-target GLP-1 effect. Fiber adds bulk. Magnesium and PEG draw water into the stool osmotically.
Basis
Inferred: advisory management guidance for GLP-1 drugs.
Downside
Magnesium can cause diarrhea or build up when kidney function is reduced.
CSmall, low-fat meals; stop eating at satiety7
Why
Reduces nausea and diarrhea, which are worse with large or high-fat meals.
Mechanism
Delayed gastric emptying plus a large gastric load causes distension and nausea.
Basis
Inferred: advisory guidance.
Downside
Watch that protein targets are still met.

Complementary interventions

EAmylin agonist (cagrilintide)8
Why
Separate satiety pathway (amylin receptor). The catalog sells a GLP3-R/cagrilintide blend.
Mechanism
Amylin acts on the area postrema and hypothalamus, which complements GLP-1R signaling.
Basis
Additive effect is demonstrated only with semaglutide (CagriSema REDEFINE 1: −20.4% vs −3.0% placebo at 68 wk, above either drug alone). No retatrutide + cagrilintide data.
Downside
Overlapping GI effects. The combination dose with retatrutide is untested.
BMetformin (background, T2D)2
Why
The phase 2 T2D trial ran on background metformin.
Mechanism
Hepatic glucose output suppression; a different pathway from incretin agonism.
Basis
Co-administration is studied (phase 2 T2D). This is safety/compatibility evidence, not proof of an additive benefit.
Downside
GI effects can stack. Long-term metformin lowers B12, which overlaps the micronutrient item above.
BCreatine monohydrate with resistance training11
Why
Helps protect lean mass while drug-driven weight loss strips muscle as well as fat.
Mechanism
Raises muscle creatine stores, supporting training volume and lean-mass gains.
Basis
Strong evidence for lean mass with resistance training (ISSN 2017). Not tested together with GLP-1 drugs; inferred.
Downside
Water weight gain (scale noise); caution with existing kidney disease.

A plausible mechanism doesn't prove synergy. Nothing here is called synergistic without combination data.

Redundancies & overlap

Semaglutide (GLP1-S), tirzepatide (GLP2-T), mazdutide, survodutideSame incretin receptors (GLP-1R ± GIPR ± GCGR). Stacking adds GI and heart-rate burden with no evidence of added benefit. Treat as alternatives, not add-ons.
CagrilintideNot redundant: a different receptor (amylin). See the complementary section.
5-Amino-1MQ, SLU-PP-332Claimed energy-expenditure effects overlap the GCGR arm in purpose only. Their evidence is preclinical (D); no combination data.

Specific cautions

  • BHeart rate: dose-dependent rise peaking near week 24. Relevant with tachyarrhythmia or when taken with stimulants.1
  • BPancreatitis: 1 serious acute case in phase 2. Asymptomatic amylase/lipase rises were also seen.1
  • BDysesthesia / skin sensitivity: 7% in phase 2. Specific to this compound so far, not a known GLP-1 class effect.1
  • AHypoglycemia with insulin or sulfonylureas (glucose-dependent insulin secretion adds to their effect). By class analogy.9
  • AMedullary thyroid carcinoma / MEN 2: the GLP-1 class carries a boxed warning based on rodent C-cell findings. No MTC or C-cell hyperplasia was seen in phase 2.9
  • ADelayed gastric emptying: slows absorption of oral drugs and raises aspiration risk under anesthesia. The tirzepatide label advises a non-oral or backup contraceptive for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase (GIP/GLP-1 analogy).9
  • ARapid weight loss raises gallbladder-event risk (GLP-1 class labeling).9

Research gaps

  • Peer-reviewed phase 3 papers (results so far are topline).
  • Body composition: lean vs fat mass lost on retatrutide.
  • Long-term significance of the heart-rate rise. A dedicated cardiovascular-outcomes result hasn't been reported.
  • Mechanism and long-term course of the dysesthesia.
  • Weight regain after stopping. The catalog's 4-on/4-off cycling is untested.
  • Any combination with cagrilintide or other agonists.
  • Identity, purity, sterility and stability of research powder vs the trial drug product. Nothing here assumes they're equivalent.
  • Pregnancy and lactation.
