GHK-Cu

Copper tripeptide-1

Catalog: GHK-Cu · Repair and matrix peptides · Evidence ceiling C

A Approved clinical use   B Human interventional   C Human observational / expert   D Preclinical   E Community / vendor   N/E Insufficient evidence

Mechanism

  • CModulates collagen, elastin and glycosaminoglycan production and wound-healing gene programs; anti-inflammatory. Mostly from cell, animal and cosmetic studies.1

Identity

AliasesCopper tripeptide-1, glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine copper
ClassNaturally occurring tripeptide–copper complex
Regulatory statusA widely used cosmetic ingredient (topical). Not an approved drug; injection is not established.
Supplier formulation100 mg powder (catalog; also sold for topical use).

Intended effects

  • CSkin aging, wound healing (topical)1
  • ESystemic 'regeneration' (injected; community use)3

Secondary findings

    Human evidence

    • CTopical: the reviews report improved aging skin (fewer wrinkles, less sagging and pigmentation, more collagen) and a strong safety record in cosmetics. The underlying trials are mostly older and small; not itemized here.1
    • N/ENo human studies of injected GHK-Cu were found.1

    Preclinical findings

    • DAnimal wound-healing and tissue-repair studies (skin, lung connective tissue, bone, liver, stomach lining).2

    Documented dosing & cycles

    E Catalog protocol (researchdosing.com)3

    • 100 mg + 5 mL BAC water
    • 5–10 units (1–2 mg) once daily, 7 days a week; start at 5 units
    • 4–12 week cycle, then 4–12 week washout
    • Catalog warns injections can sting; it suggests diluting each dose

    Preserved from the catalog as written. This is a vendor protocol, not a clinical recommendation.

    Formulation math: U-100 syringe units

    Units = dose ÷ concentration × 100 (100 units = 1 mL). "Catalog" = what researchdosing.com prints; ⚠ = off by more than rounding (0.6 units or 5%). Over 100 units = split the injection. The math says nothing about sterility, compatibility or stability after mixing.

    Optimization & support

    Foundational support

    AVitamin C sufficiency (RDA 75 mg women / 90 mg men)4
    Why
    Collagen can't be made properly without vitamin C.
    Mechanism
    Cofactor for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases that stabilize collagen.
    Basis
    Established nutrition science (NIH ODS). Extra beyond sufficiency isn't shown to boost repair-peptide effects.
    Downside
    Very high doses can cause GI upset and raise kidney-stone risk in susceptible people (NIH ODS).

    Mechanistic support

    BHydrolyzed collagen peptides daily5
    Why
    The best-supported oral supplement for skin hydration and elasticity.
    Mechanism
    Collagen-derived peptides (e.g. Pro-Hyp) are absorbed and signal fibroblasts; also supply amino acids for matrix.
    Basis
    Meta-analyses of 19–26 RCTs: better hydration and elasticity; wrinkle reduction not robust. Not tested with GHK-Cu or other peptides.
    Downside
    Benefit builds over weeks; duration mattered more than dose.

    Complementary interventions

    A plausible mechanism doesn't prove synergy. Nothing here is called synergistic without combination data.

    Redundancies & overlap

    GLOW / KLOW blendsAlready contain GHK-Cu.
    Topical GHK-Cu creamsThe evidence-backed route for skin.

    Specific cautions

    • EInjection-site pain and reactions; the catalog itself describes stinging from free copper.3
    • DSystemic copper load from daily injections isn't characterized (inferred).1

    Research gaps

    • Any human injection data.
    • Controlled topical trials (not itemized in the reviews retrieved).
