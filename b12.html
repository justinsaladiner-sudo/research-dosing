Vitamin B12

Cobalamin (catalog: methylcobalamin)

Catalog: B12 · Cellular energy and antioxidants · Evidence ceiling A

A Approved clinical use   B Human interventional   C Human observational / expert   D Preclinical   E Community / vendor   N/E Insufficient evidence

Mechanism

  • ARequired for DNA synthesis (red-cell production) and myelin maintenance. Deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia and nerve damage (subacute combined degeneration).1

Identity

AliasesMethylcobalamin (catalog), cyanocobalamin (US-approved injection)
ClassEssential vitamin; cofactor for methionine synthase and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase
Regulatory statusCyanocobalamin injection is FDA-approved for B12 deficiency, including pernicious anemia. Methylcobalamin injection isn't an approved US product.
Supplier formulationPre-made solution. The catalog doesn't state the vial size; '50 units = 5,000 mcg' implies 10 mg/mL.

Intended effects

  • ATreating B12 deficiency, including pernicious anemia and malabsorption1
  • EEnergy (community use, when not deficient)2

Secondary findings

  • AAt 1,000 mcg injected, 50–98% appears in the urine: excess beyond what the body binds is excreted.1

Human evidence

  • ALabel (pernicious anemia): 100 mcg daily for 6–7 days IM or deep SC, then tapering, then 100 mcg monthly for life.1
  • N/ENo good evidence that extra B12 raises energy in people who aren't deficient. That claim wasn't found in the label or any trial reviewed.1

Preclinical findings

    Documented dosing & cycles

    A Label dosing (cyanocobalamin, deficiency)1

    100 mcgDaily × 6–7 days
    →
    100 mcgMonthly, for life

    The label says to avoid the IV route. The catalog's 5,000 mcg doses are 50× the label's monthly dose.

    E Catalog protocol (researchdosing.com)2

    • 5,000 mcg once weekly for 4 weeks (or 10 weeks)
    • Then 2,500–5,000 mcg once or twice a month, indefinitely
    • IM preferred; SC acceptable
    • Morning; with or without food

    Preserved from the catalog as written. This is a vendor protocol, not a clinical recommendation.

    Catalog discrepancies (flagged, record unchanged)

    • Vial size isn't stated; 10 mg/mL is implied by the catalog's own unit math.
    • The catalog says not to use supplemental B12 with chronic kidney problems. That isn't a label contraindication (the label lists cobalt sensitivity).

    Formulation math: U-100 syringe units

    Units = dose ÷ concentration × 100 (100 units = 1 mL). "Catalog" = what researchdosing.com prints; ⚠ = off by more than rounding (0.6 units or 5%). Over 100 units = split the injection. The math says nothing about sterility, compatibility or stability after mixing.

    ✓ Every unit value the catalog prints matches the computed value.

    10 mg vial + 1 mL pre-made solution (10 mg/mL implied) = 10 mg/mL

    Optimization & support

    Foundational support

    Mechanistic support

    ATest before treating (B12, plus MMA if borderline)1
    Why
    B12 corrects a deficiency; it doesn't boost a normal level.
    Mechanism
    Megaloblastic anemia and neuropathy come only from deficiency.
    Basis
    Label indications are deficiency states.
    Downside
    None.

    Complementary interventions

    A plausible mechanism doesn't prove synergy. Nothing here is called synergistic without combination data.

    Redundancies & overlap

    LIPO-B / LIPO-C (catalog)These already contain B12.

    Specific cautions

    • AContraindicated with cobalt or B12 sensitivity.1
    • AEarly Leber's disease (hereditary optic atrophy): cyanocobalamin caused severe, rapid optic atrophy.1
    • ALow potassium and sudden death can occur when severe megaloblastic anemia is treated intensively.1

    Research gaps

    • Benefit of 5,000 mcg weekly in people who aren't deficient.
    • The catalog's methylcobalamin solution: source, sterility and concentration aren't documented.
