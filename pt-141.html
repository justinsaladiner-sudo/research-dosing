Bremelanotide (PT-141)

Melanocortin receptor agonist

Catalog: PT-141 · Hormone and reproductive signaling · Evidence ceiling A

A Approved clinical use   B Human interventional   C Human observational / expert   D Preclinical   E Community / vendor   N/E Insufficient evidence

Mechanism

  • AActivates central melanocortin (MC4) receptors involved in sexual desire. Acts on the brain, not on blood flow like PDE5 inhibitors.1

Identity

AliasesVyleesi (approved autoinjector)
ClassCyclic heptapeptide melanocortin agonist, structurally related to melanotan II
TargetsMelanocortin receptors, chiefly MC4R (central)
Regulatory statusFDA-approved (Vyleesi) only for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder. Not approved for men or postmenopausal women.
Supplier formulationLyophilized research powder; 10 mg vial (catalog).

Intended effects

  • AHypoactive sexual desire disorder in premenopausal women1
  • ESexual function in men (off-label, community)2

Secondary findings

  • ATransient blood-pressure rise and heart-rate drop after each dose, usually resolving within 12 h.1
  • AFocal hyperpigmentation (face, gums, breasts) in 1% at up to 8 doses/month; more with daily dosing and darker skin; not always resolved.1

Human evidence

  • APhase 3 (pooled): nausea 40% (21% after the first dose, then declining to about 3%); flushing 20% vs <1%.1
  • AStopping for adverse events: nausea 8%, headache 2%, vomiting 1%, flushing 1%.1
  • AStop after 8 weeks if there's no improvement (label).1

    Documented dosing & cycles

    A Label dosing (Vyleesi)1

    1.75 mgAs needed, ≥45 min before sex

    At most 1 dose per 24 hours, and more than 8 doses a month isn't recommended.

    E Catalog protocol (researchdosing.com)2

    • Test dose 0.5 mg first, to check tolerance
    • Standard 2 mg, 45–60 minutes before sex; up to 2.5 mg
    • Max 8 standard doses (16 mg) per month

    Preserved from the catalog as written. This is a vendor protocol, not a clinical recommendation.

    Catalog discrepancies (flagged, record unchanged)

    • The catalog's standard dose is 2–2.5 mg. The approved dose is 1.75 mg.
    • The catalog's monthly cap (8 doses) matches the label's frequency limit.
    • Unit math: every unit value the catalog prints matches (5 mg/mL).

    Formulation math: U-100 syringe units

    Units = dose ÷ concentration × 100 (100 units = 1 mL). "Catalog" = what researchdosing.com prints; ⚠ = off by more than rounding (0.6 units or 5%). Over 100 units = split the injection. The math says nothing about sterility, compatibility or stability after mixing.

    Optimization & support

    Foundational support

    Mechanistic support

    AAnti-nausea plan for the first doses1
    Why
    Nausea hits 21% after the first dose and fades with repeated use.
    Mechanism
    Central melanocortin activation; tolerance develops.
    Basis
    Demonstrated: the label's nausea time course; it advises anti-emetics for persistent nausea.
    Downside
    Some anti-emetics have their own interactions.

    Complementary interventions

    A plausible mechanism doesn't prove synergy. Nothing here is called synergistic without combination data.

    Redundancies & overlap

    Melanotan IISame melanocortin family; non-selective; tanning plus libido; unapproved.
    PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil etc.)Different mechanism (blood flow). Not redundant, but their combined blood-pressure effects aren't covered by the label.

    Specific cautions

    • AContraindicated: uncontrolled hypertension or known cardiovascular disease.1
    • AOral naltrexone: bremelanotide can cut its exposure significantly. Avoid with naltrexone for alcohol or opioid addiction.1
    • ASlows gastric emptying: may delay oral drugs that need a fast onset.1
    • AFocal hyperpigmentation, more likely with frequent dosing.1

    Research gaps

    • Efficacy and safety in men (not approved; the catalog markets it broadly).
    • Doses above 1.75 mg.
