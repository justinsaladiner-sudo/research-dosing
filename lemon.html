Lemon Bottle

Cosmetic 'fat-dissolving' solution

Catalog: Lemon Bottle · Lipotropics · Evidence ceiling E

A Approved clinical use   B Human interventional   C Human observational / expert   D Preclinical   E Community / vendor   N/E Insufficient evidence

Mechanism

  • ENo proven mechanism. Swissmedic: 'No medicinal effect has been scientifically proven.'1

Identity

Declared ingredientsMainly bromelain, lecithin, riboflavin plus botanicals (packaging). The catalog page also says deoxycholic acid; a clinician source says it contains none.
ClassCosmetic product marketed for injection lipolysis
Regulatory statusUK: classed as a cosmetic, not a medicine (MHRA). Switzerland (Swissmedic) and Finland (Fimea/Tukes) warned against it in 2024; Finland notes cosmetics can't be used in injection therapies.
Supplier formulationPre-made solution; injected 1–2 mL per point in a grid.

Intended effects

  • ELocal fat reduction (cosmetic marketing)3

Secondary findings

  • CReported harms: severe bruising, infections, abscesses, and one case of necrosis.1

Human evidence

  • CNo clinical trials. Swissmedic's lab found the composition didn't match the declaration; some samples contained none of the listed ingredients.1
  • AThe approved alternative: deoxycholic acid (Kybella) is FDA-approved only for submental (under-chin) fat. In its trials, 4% had temporary nerve injury (asymmetric smile), 72% bruising, and 2% difficulty swallowing.2

Preclinical findings

    Documented dosing & cycles

    E Catalog protocol (researchdosing.com)3

    • Map the area in a grid; inject 1 mL (thin areas), 1.5 mL (moderate) or 2 mL (dense fat) per point
    • 3–5 sessions, exactly 7 days apart
    • Limits: 10–20 mL per session for small areas; 30 mL per area; 100 mL per session overall

    Preserved from the catalog as written. This is a vendor protocol, not a clinical recommendation.

    Catalog discrepancies (flagged, record unchanged)

    • The catalog says it contains deoxycholic acid. The packaging lists bromelain, lecithin and riboflavin, and a clinician source says there's no deoxycholic acid.
    • Regulators classify it as a cosmetic (UK) or warn against injecting it (Switzerland, Finland), yet the catalog gives an injection protocol.

    Formulation math: U-100 syringe units

    Units = dose ÷ concentration × 100 (100 units = 1 mL). "Catalog" = what researchdosing.com prints; ⚠ = off by more than rounding (0.6 units or 5%). Over 100 units = split the injection. The math says nothing about sterility, compatibility or stability after mixing.
