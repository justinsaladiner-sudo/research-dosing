What is Bremelanotide (PT-141)?

Bremelanotide (PT-141) activates melanocortin receptors in the brain involved in sexual desire. It's FDA-approved as Vyleesi, 1.75 mg as needed, for premenopausal women with low sexual desire. Nausea and a temporary blood-pressure rise are its main effects.

Evidence ceiling A

  • AHypoactive sexual desire disorder in premenopausal women
  • ESexual function in men (off-label, community)

Central action

MC4 receptors in the brain, not blood flow.

Clinical evidence

  • APhase 3 (pooled): nausea 40% (21% after the first dose, then declining to about 3%); flushing 20% vs <1%.
  • AStopping for adverse events: nausea 8%, headache 2%, vomiting 1%, flushing 1%.
  • AStop after 8 weeks if there's no improvement (label).

Common side effects

  • Nausea 40% (21% first dose, fades)
  • Flushing 20%
  • Transient blood-pressure rise
  • Focal hyperpigmentation 1%

Important considerations

  • Contraindicated: uncontrolled hypertension or known cardiovascular disease
  • Oral naltrexone: bremelanotide can cut its exposure significantly
  • Slows gastric emptying: may delay oral drugs that need a fast onset
  • Focal hyperpigmentation, more likely with frequent dosing

Key notes (catalog)

  • Test dose 0.5 mg first, to check tolerance
  • Standard 2 mg, 45–60 minutes before sex; up to 2.5 mg
  • Max 8 standard doses (16 mg) per month

Reconstitution example

Add 2 mL BAC water → 5 mg/mL

  1. Swab both vial tops with an alcohol pad
  2. Draw the BAC water (3 mL syringe, 22–23G)
  3. Inject slowly into the peptide vial
  4. Roll gently between your palms; don't shake
  5. Let it sit 15–30 min until fully dissolved

Catalog: refrigerate reconstituted vials in an opaque, airtight container, not in the door. Use within 60–90 days of mixing (catalog claim, not stability data). Powder: room temperature 30–60 days, fridge up to 1 year.
