KPV

α-MSH C-terminal tripeptide (Lys-Pro-Val)

Catalog: KPV · Repair and matrix peptides · Evidence ceiling D

A Approved clinical use   B Human interventional   C Human observational / expert   D Preclinical   E Community / vendor   N/E Insufficient evidence

Mechanism

  • DTaken up by gut cells through the PepT1 transporter (more active in inflamed gut), where it reduces NF-κB inflammatory signaling.1

Identity

AliasesLys-Pro-Val, α-MSH(11–13)
ClassAnti-inflammatory tripeptide from the end of α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone
Regulatory statusNot approved; no human trials found.
Supplier formulationResearch powder. The catalog doesn't state the vial size; its unit math implies 10 mg in 2.5 mL.

Intended effects

  • DInflammatory bowel disease (oral, preclinical)1
  • EGeneral anti-inflammatory use (injected; community)2

Secondary findings

    Human evidence

    • N/ENo human studies found.1

    Preclinical findings

    • DDalmasso 2008: oral KPV (in drinking water) reduced colitis in DSS and TNBS mouse models, via PepT1.1

    Documented dosing & cycles

    E Catalog protocol (researchdosing.com)2

    • Mix with 2.5 mL BAC water
    • Start 200 mcg once daily; increase by '100 mcg' (3 units) steps as needed; max 1 mg
    • 8-week cycle, then 4–8 week washout

    Preserved from the catalog as written. This is a vendor protocol, not a clinical recommendation.

    Catalog discrepancies (flagged, record unchanged)

    • Vial size isn't stated; 10 mg is implied.
    • The catalog's own footnote: a '3-unit' step is really 120 mcg, not 100 mcg (100 mcg is 2.5 units).

    Formulation math: U-100 syringe units

    Units = dose ÷ concentration × 100 (100 units = 1 mL). "Catalog" = what researchdosing.com prints; ⚠ = off by more than rounding (0.6 units or 5%). Over 100 units = split the injection. The math says nothing about sterility, compatibility or stability after mixing.
