What is Retatrutide?

Retatrutide is a single peptide that activates three gut-hormone receptors: GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon. GLP-1 and GIP reduce appetite and improve blood-sugar control. The glucagon arm is thought to raise energy use and liver fat burning. In trials it has produced larger average weight loss than any approved GLP-1 drug.

Clinical evidence

  • BPhase 2 obesity (n=338, 48 wk, SC weekly): −24.2% at 12 mg vs −2.1% placebo
  • BPhase 2 T2D (on metformin): HbA1c −2.02% at 24 wk on 12 mg vs −1.41% dulaglutide 1.5 mg and −0.01% placebo
  • BTRIUMPH-1 phase 3 (n=2,339, no diabetes, 80 wk): −19.0% (4 mg), −25.9% (9 mg), −28.3% (12 mg). −30.3% at 104 wk in the BMI ≥35 extension (n=532).
  • BTRIUMPH-2 (T2D, 80 wk): Lilly topline −12.7% / −19.1% / −20.8% at 4 / 9 / 12 mg, A1C down up to 1.6%
  • BTRIUMPH-3 (severe obesity + established CVD, 80 wk): up to −22.6%

Common side effects

  • Nausea: 42% at 12 mg (15% placebo)
  • Diarrhea: 32%
  • Constipation: 26%
  • Vomiting: 25%
  • Skin sensitivity: 7% (phase 2)
  • Heart rate rise, peaking around week 24
  • Mostly mild to moderate; fewer with a 2 mg start

Important considerations

  • Heart rate: dose-dependent rise peaking near week 24
  • Pancreatitis: 1 serious acute case in phase 2
  • Dysesthesia / skin sensitivity: 7% in phase 2
  • Hypoglycemia with insulin or sulfonylureas (glucose-dependent insulin secretion adds to their effect)
  • Medullary thyroid carcinoma / MEN 2: the GLP-1 class carries a boxed warning based on rodent C-cell findings
  • Delayed gastric emptying: slows absorption of oral drugs and raises aspiration risk under anesthesia

Key notes (catalog)

  • GLP-naive: start at 2 mg per week
  • Prior GLP-1 use with at least one dose increase: start at 4 mg per week
  • Every 7 days, same day each week; no fasting needed
  • Cycle optional: 4 months on, 4 months off
  • No escalation schedule given; unit tables run to 12 mg

Reconstitution example

Add 2.5 mL BAC water → 12 mg/mL

  1. Swab both vial tops with an alcohol pad
  2. Draw the BAC water (3 mL syringe, 22–23G)
  3. Inject slowly into the peptide vial
  4. Roll gently between your palms; don't shake
  5. Let it sit 15–30 min until fully dissolved

Catalog: refrigerate reconstituted vials in an opaque, airtight container, not in the door. Use within 60–90 days of mixing (catalog claim, not stability data). Powder: room temperature 30–60 days, fridge up to 1 year.

Common combinations

  • Amylin agonist (cagrilintide) E
  • Metformin (background, T2D) B
  • Creatine monohydrate with resistance training B
  • Protein 1.2–1.6 g/kg/d during active loss C
  • Resistance training ≥3×/wk + ≥150 min/wk aerobic B

Avoid / use caution with

  • Insulin or sulfonylureas (hypoglycemia)
  • Oral contraceptives: absorption is reduced; use a backup method for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase
  • Other GLP-1 / GIP agonists (stacked GI effects)
  • Stimulants or tachyarrhythmia (heart rate rises)
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Upcoming anesthesia (aspiration risk from delayed emptying)

Similar options

  • Semaglutide (GLP1-S), tirzepatide (GLP2-T), mazdutide, survodutide: Same incretin receptors (GLP-1R ± GIPR ± GCGR)
  • Cagrilintide: Not redundant: a different receptor (amylin)
  • 5-Amino-1MQ, SLU-PP-332: Claimed energy-expenditure effects overlap the GCGR arm in purpose only

Research gaps

  • Peer-reviewed phase 3 papers (results so far are topline).
  • Body composition: lean vs fat mass lost on retatrutide.
  • Long-term significance of the heart-rate rise. A dedicated cardiovascular-outcomes result hasn't been reported.
  • Mechanism and long-term course of the dysesthesia.
  • Weight regain after stopping. The catalog's 4-on/4-off cycling is untested.
