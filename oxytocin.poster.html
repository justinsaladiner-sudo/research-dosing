What is Oxytocin?

Oxytocin is the pituitary hormone behind labor contractions and milk let-down, and it also acts on the brain's social circuits. IV oxytocin (Pitocin) is a standard obstetric drug. A large trial of intranasal oxytocin for autism found no benefit, and daily injections haven't been studied.

Clinical evidence

  • BSikich 2021 (NEJM, children and adolescents 3–17 with autism, intranasal, target 48 IU/day, 24 weeks): no benefit over placebo on social or cognitive measures.
  • N/ENo peer-reviewed human study of daily subcutaneous oxytocin was found.

Common side effects

  • Water retention / low sodium (antidiuretic effect)
  • Uterine contractions
  • Nausea, headache (intranasal studies)

Important considerations

  • Antidiuretic effect: water intoxication is possible at high doses or with heavy fluid intake (label)
  • Contracts the uterus: avoid in pregnancy outside obstetric care
  • The label notes inherent pressor (blood-pressure) properties at large doses

Key notes (catalog)

  • Mix 10 mg with 2.5 mL BAC water; use 30- or 50-unit syringes
  • Start at 100 mcg once daily; up to 200 mcg daily
  • 8-week cycle, then 4-week washout

Reconstitution example

Add 2.5 mL BAC water → 4 mg/mL

  1. Swab both vial tops with an alcohol pad
  2. Draw the BAC water (3 mL syringe, 22–23G)
  3. Inject slowly into the peptide vial
  4. Roll gently between your palms; don't shake
  5. Let it sit 15–30 min until fully dissolved

Catalog: refrigerate reconstituted vials in an opaque, airtight container, not in the door. Use within 60–90 days of mixing (catalog claim, not stability data). Powder: room temperature 30–60 days, fridge up to 1 year.
