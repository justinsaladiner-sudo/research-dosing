VIP

Vasoactive intestinal peptide

Catalog: VIP · Immune and other signaling peptides · Evidence ceiling A

A Approved clinical use   B Human interventional   C Human observational / expert   D Preclinical   E Community / vendor   N/E Insufficient evidence

Mechanism

  • BRelaxes vascular and airway smooth muscle (vasodilation, bronchodilation) and dampens inflammation through VPAC receptors.1

Identity

AliasesAviptadil (synthetic VIP)
Class28-amino-acid neuropeptide (glucagon/secretin family)
TargetsVPAC1 and VPAC2 receptors (smooth muscle, lung, immune cells)
Regulatory statusAviptadil + phentolamine (Invicorp) is approved in the UK and parts of Europe as an intracavernosal ED injection. IV aviptadil for COVID respiratory failure was denied US authorization.
Supplier formulationLyophilized research powder. The catalog doesn't state the vial size; its unit math implies 10 mg.

Intended effects

  • AErectile dysfunction (intracavernosal, with phentolamine)2
  • BCOVID-19 respiratory failure (IV; trials negative/futile)1
  • EInflammation, mold illness / CIRS (community use)3

Secondary findings

    Human evidence

    • BIV aviptadil, critical COVID-19 (196 patients): primary endpoint not significant, though a secondary survival odds ratio was. NIH ACTIV-3b (461 patients) stopped early for futility. FDA declined emergency use authorization.1
    • AInvicorp (aviptadil + phentolamine) is approved for ED not responding to oral PDE5 inhibitors (UK, 2015).2
    • N/ENo human data for daily SC VIP.1

    Preclinical findings

      Documented dosing & cycles

      E Catalog protocol (researchdosing.com)3

      • Mix with 2.5 mL (or 2 mL) BAC water
      • 100 mcg (week 1) → 150 mcg (weeks 2–3) → 200 mcg (weeks 4–8), once daily, 5 days/week
      • Morning or early afternoon, fasted
      • 8-week cycle + 2–4 week washout, or 3 months on + 1 month off

      Preserved from the catalog as written. This is a vendor protocol, not a clinical recommendation.

      Catalog discrepancies (flagged, record unchanged)

      • Vial size isn't stated; 10 mg is implied by the unit math.
      • 100 mcg = 2.5 units and 150 mcg = 3.75 units; the catalog prints 3 and 4 (rounding). Other values match.

      Formulation math: U-100 syringe units

      Units = dose ÷ concentration × 100 (100 units = 1 mL). "Catalog" = what researchdosing.com prints; ⚠ = off by more than rounding (0.6 units or 5%). Over 100 units = split the injection. The math says nothing about sterility, compatibility or stability after mixing.
