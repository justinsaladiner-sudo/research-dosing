What is Vitamin B12?

Vitamin B12 is needed to make red blood cells and maintain nerves. Injections are FDA-approved to treat deficiency, including pernicious anemia, at 100 mcg a month. The catalog's 5,000 mcg doses are 50 times that, and most of an excess dose is passed in urine.

Evidence ceiling A

Energy claim

Clinical evidence

  • ALabel (pernicious anemia): 100 mcg daily for 6–7 days IM or deep SC, then tapering, then 100 mcg monthly for life.
  • N/ENo good evidence that extra B12 raises energy in people who aren't deficient

Common side effects

  • Injection-site pain
  • Rare hypersensitivity (cobalt)

Important considerations

  • Contraindicated with cobalt or B12 sensitivity
  • Early Leber's disease (hereditary optic atrophy): cyanocobalamin caused severe, rapid optic atrophy
  • Low potassium and sudden death can occur when severe megaloblastic anemia is treated intensively

Key notes (catalog)

  • 5,000 mcg once weekly for 4 weeks (or 10 weeks)
  • Then 2,500–5,000 mcg once or twice a month, indefinitely
  • IM preferred; SC acceptable
  • Morning; with or without food
