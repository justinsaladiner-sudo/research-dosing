What is Cagrilintide?

Cagrilintide is a once-weekly long-acting amylin analog. Amylin is a satiety hormone released with insulin, and it acts on a different pathway from GLP-1. Alone it produced about 11% weight loss in phase 2. Paired with semaglutide (CagriSema), it reached about 20% in phase 3.

Clinical evidence

  • BPhase 2 (Lau 2021, 26 wk, 0.3–4.5 mg weekly): −6.0% to −10.8% vs −3.0% placebo. 4.5 mg beat liraglutide 3.0 mg (−10.8% vs −9.0%)
  • BREDEFINE 1 (n=3,417, 68 wk): CagriSema 2.4/2.4 mg −20.4% vs −3.0% placebo (treatment policy); −22.7% in adherent participants
  • BREDEFINE 2 (T2D): CagriSema −13.7% vs −3.4% placebo at 68 wk.
  • BGI adverse events in phase 2: 41–63% vs 32% placebo, mainly nausea (20–47% vs 18%).

Common side effects

  • Nausea: 20–47% vs 18% placebo (phase 2)
  • Other GI effects, dose-related
  • Injection-site reactions
  • Combined with semaglutide: more GI effects than either alone

Important considerations

  • GI effects are dose-related
  • With insulin: the amylin analog pramlintide carries a boxed warning for severe insulin-induced hypoglycemia (class analogy)
  • Slowed gastric emptying: aspiration risk under anesthesia (class analogy)

Key notes (catalog)

Reconstitution example

Add 2 mL BAC water → 5 mg/mL

  1. Swab both vial tops with an alcohol pad
  2. Draw the BAC water (3 mL syringe, 22–23G)
  3. Inject slowly into the peptide vial
  4. Roll gently between your palms; don't shake
  5. Let it sit 15–30 min until fully dissolved

Catalog: refrigerate reconstituted vials in an opaque, airtight container, not in the door. Use within 60–90 days of mixing (catalog claim, not stability data). Powder: room temperature 30–60 days, fridge up to 1 year.

Common combinations

Avoid / use caution with

  • Insulin (severe hypoglycemia with amylin analogs)
  • Fast escalation (GI effects)
  • Upcoming anesthesia (slowed gastric emptying)
  • Untested stacks with tirzepatide or retatrutide

Similar options

  • Pramlintide (Symlin): Same amylin pathway; short-acting, mealtime, approved for insulin users
  • GLP-1 / GIP agonists: Not redundant: different receptors

Research gaps

  • No approved product or label.
  • Monotherapy beyond 26 weeks.
  • The catalog's 8-week cycle and weekly escalation have no trial basis.
  • Combinations with tirzepatide or retatrutide (the catalog sells a GLP3-R/cagrilintide blend).
